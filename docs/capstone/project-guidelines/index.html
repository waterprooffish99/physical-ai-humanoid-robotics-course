Capstone Project: Guidelines

Introduction​

The Capstone Project is a significant academic undertaking that requires careful planning, rigorous execution, and clear communication. To ensure a consistent and high-quality experience for all students, this chapter outlines the comprehensive guidelines and expectations for your Capstone Project. Adherence to these guidelines is crucial for successful project completion, effective evaluation, and maintaining academic integrity. This document will detail the process from proposal submission to final presentation, providing clarity on deliverables, evaluation criteria, and available resources.

Topics​

Project Proposal Submission​

The project proposal is the foundational document for your Capstone Project. It formally defines your project scope, objectives, and approach.

  • Submission Deadline: [Human Task: Specify the deadline for proposal submission.]
  • Elements of the Proposal:
    • Project Title: A clear and concise title reflecting your project's focus.
    • Abstract/Overview: A brief summary (150-250 words) of your project, its significance, and expected outcomes.
    • Problem Statement: Clearly articulate the problem your project aims to solve.
    • Objectives: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) objectives for your project.
    • Methodology: Outline the technical approach, tools, and techniques you plan to use.
    • Expected Outcomes: Describe the anticipated results or deliverables of your project.
    • Preliminary Timeline: A high-level plan for project milestones and deadlines.
    • Team Members and Roles: If applicable, specify team members and their responsibilities.

Technical Report Structure​

A well-structured technical report is essential for documenting your project's journey, methodology, and results. The report should be written in a professional, academic style.

  • Required Sections:
    • Title Page: Project title, author(s), date, course name.
    • Abstract: (Same as proposal, potentially refined).
    • Introduction: Background, problem, motivation, project objectives.
    • Literature Review: Survey of existing work and technologies relevant to your project.
    • Design: Detailed description of your system architecture, components, and design choices.
    • Implementation: Explanation of how the system was built, including hardware/software details, algorithms used, and challenges faced.
    • Results: Presentation of experimental results, performance evaluations, and project outcomes. Use figures, tables, and graphs where appropriate.
    • Discussion: Interpretation of results, comparison with objectives, limitations, and potential future work.
    • Conclusion: Summary of achievements and key takeaways.
    • References: A comprehensive list of all sources cited.
    • Appendices: (Optional) Supplemental material like code snippets, detailed schematics, or additional data.

Presentation and Demonstration​

The final presentation and demonstration are opportunities to showcase your work and communicate its significance to a broader audience.

  • Format: [Human Task: Specify presentation format, e.g., 15-minute presentation followed by 5-minute Q&A.]
  • Key Content:
    • Introduction to the problem and project goals.
    • Overview of the system design and implementation.
    • Demonstration of the functional prototype (physical or simulated).
    • Discussion of results, challenges, and lessons learned.
    • Future work and potential impact.
  • Demonstration: Ensure your demonstration is well-rehearsed, highlights key functionalities, and is robust against potential technical issues. Be prepared to explain the underlying code or hardware if asked.

Subtopics​

Ethical Considerations in Project Work​

Students are expected to consider the ethical implications of their Capstone Project. This includes:

  • Data Privacy: If your project involves data collection, ensure compliance with privacy regulations.
  • Responsible AI Development: Consider potential biases, fairness, and transparency of AI components.
  • Intellectual Property: Understand and respect intellectual property rights related to code, designs, and research.
  • Safety: If working with physical robots, ensure all safety protocols are followed.

Collaboration Best Practices​

For team projects, effective collaboration is crucial. Best practices include:

  • Clear Role Definition: Assign specific roles and responsibilities to each team member.
  • Regular Communication: Schedule regular meetings to discuss progress, challenges, and next steps.
  • Version Control: Use a version control system (e.g., Git) for collaborative code development.
  • Conflict Resolution: Establish mechanisms for resolving disagreements constructively.

Resources and Support​

You are not alone in this journey. Various resources and support mechanisms are available:

  • Faculty Advisors: Your primary point of contact for academic guidance and project direction.
  • Technical Mentors: Individuals with expertise in specific technologies or domains who can offer practical advice.
  • Library Resources: Access to academic databases, research papers, and textbooks.
  • Lab Facilities/Equipment: Availability of specialized hardware or software for your project.
  • Online Communities: Relevant forums, documentation, and open-source communities.
